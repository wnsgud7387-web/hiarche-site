The first subscriber

Arche, a one-person company building apps with an AI team · Part 4

Day 56 (as of Nov 10) · What didn't work: No. 2

As I wrote at the end of Part 3, from September 30 the apps could take money. The next day I started five days of search ads, and stopped at eight installs.

Before those five days were over, one person came.

October 2, 5:53 a.m.

The first monthly subscription came in. One person. It was for Niblore.

I'll never forget that first subscription. Someone paid for an app I thought wasn't good enough. I was so grateful, and I'll never forget it.

All I know about that person is one fact: they decided to pay every month.

After the first one

The numbers after that were quiet.

About ten days later, on October 14, three or four people a day were actually using Niblore.

What didn't work, No. 2

Ad revenue. About $0.38 for all of October.

Putting ads inside an app did nothing on its own. There had to be people to see the ads first.

While daily users stayed at three or four, I had set the age rating to 15+, hoping as many people as possible would use the app. Then I realized that way I couldn't satisfy either minors or adults. So I set the content level for adults and boldly narrowed the audience.

The number one

One is a small number. Still, it wasn't zero.

What's left is finding more people like that one.

How did your first customer find you?
