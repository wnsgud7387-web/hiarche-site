The day we decided: overseas first

Arche, a one-person company building apps with an AI team · Part 6

Day 70 (as of Nov 24) · What didn't work: No. 4

As I wrote in Part 5, the numbers in early October were small. But a few people had found us without being called.

On October 13, I made one decision.

October 13: overseas first

That day, the company's direction became "overseas first." Not building only for Korea, but thinking about users outside it from the start.

The same day, our D-U-N-S number came through: an ID that lets a company be recognized anywhere in the world. To publish apps under a company name rather than a personal one, the app stores asked for it.

October 14: under the company's name

Around noon the next day, our Google Play account switched from personal to organization. From then on, the store listing says "Arche."

The app names were already facing outward: Niblore, Wanderlure, Chosim. Each Korean name has an English one beside it.

What didn't work, No. 4

Android. The account was ready, but to sell paid apps on Google Play in Korea you need one more number: an e-commerce business registration. Our Android launch was stuck on that one number.

It arrived at 2:22 p.m. on October 22. That afternoon, Niblore and Wanderlure went to Play review.

One door

On October 28, Niblore passed Google Play review. For Niblore, it was the first door outside the iPhone.

We'd decided to look overseas first. The first thing we actually had to clear was one piece of Korean paperwork.

Do you build for the world from day one, or start close to home?
