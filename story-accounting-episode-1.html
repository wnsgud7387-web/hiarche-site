Only 3 months belong to this year

4-minute story · Accruals and prepayments

Tomasz had been the bookkeeper at Lantern Yoga Studio for two months when Nadia, the studio manager, handed him his first year-end job: an insurance schedule and the rent payments. "Just tell me what this year really cost us," she said. "Not when the money left the bank — when we used the thing we paid for." Matching expenses to the period that uses them is the accruals concept.

One. Insurance paid in advance

On 1 October the studio paid a full year of insurance, $4,800, and the whole amount was posted to the insurance expense account. Tomasz counted the calendar: 3 months of cover had passed by 31 December and 9 were still to come.

He wrote down: insurance expense $3,600, carried forward $1,200. Nadia frowned at the sheet. "Your two figures have swapped places. This year we were covered for only 3 months, so the expense is $1,200. The other 9 months, $3,600, are cover we have already paid for and will use next year. That is a prepayment — an asset."

"So I multiplied by the months left instead of the months used." Left alone, his schedule would have shown insurance of $3,600 instead of $1,200.

Two. Rent not yet paid

Next, the rent. Cash paid during the year was $19,200. But that figure included $1,600 of last December's rent, paid late in January. And this December's rent of $2,400 had not been paid at all.

"We paid last year's rent this year, so surely it's a cost to us now? Then I add the $1,600…" Adding both balances would give rent of $23,200. Nadia shook her head. "Don't ask who paid when. Ask which year we were in the building."

She wrote on the whiteboard: rent used last year but paid this year belongs to last year — take it out. Rent used this year but not yet paid belongs to this year — put it in. An expense used but not yet paid is an accrual, and because we still owe it, it is a liability. $19,200 − $1,600 + $2,400 = $20,000.

What is the total insurance and rent expense for the year?

  • A year's insurance of $4,800 was paid on 1 October and debited to insurance expense. The year end is 31 December.
  • Rent paid in cash $19,200 · opening rent accrual $1,600 · closing rent accrual $2,400

Three. Pay day and cost day

Tomasz's corrected total for the year was insurance $1,200 plus rent $20,000: $21,200. His first draft, with both slips, would have reported $26,800. In the statement of financial position, the prepayment of $3,600 sits in current assets and the rent accrual of $2,400 sits in current liabilities. "So the day the money moves and the day the cost happens are different days," he said. Nadia initialled the schedule. "Get that right and half of year end is done."

  • Expenses follow the period of use, not the date of payment.
  • Paid in advance and not yet used: a prepayment, a current asset.
  • Used but not yet paid: an accrual, a current liability.
